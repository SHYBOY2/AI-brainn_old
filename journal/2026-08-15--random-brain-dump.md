Journal – Random Brain Dump

11 August 2026

Today, while playing Math Guru, a math puzzle game involving addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, I realized something important.

When you fail to solve a puzzle, you should remember the sequence of your previous attempts instead of repeating the same pattern. Once you recognize that a method isn't working, you need to reroute your thinking and look for a different pattern or approach.

Sometimes there is another way to reach the correct answer, even if you don't immediately understand your original mistake. However, the best outcome is not just getting the correct answer—it is understanding why your previous approach failed.

In the final puzzle, the answer depended on 19 − 11 = 8. The number 11 had been there the entire time, but I couldn't see it until I had completed all the other possibilities. When only 11 was left, I was still confused for a moment, but then it suddenly clicked.

This taught me that our minds can develop blind spots. We often keep searching using the same mental pattern, even when that pattern is wrong. Sometimes the solution has been in front of us the whole time, but we cannot see it because our thinking is trapped.

Lesson: When you're stuck, don't just try harder—change your pattern of thinking. A different perspective can reveal what was invisible before.
